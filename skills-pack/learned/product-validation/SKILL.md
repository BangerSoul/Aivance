# Product Validation

Business thinking for builders: validate through **selling, not building**. Most
products fail because the builder spent months building something nobody wants.

## Core Principle

**Validation happens through selling, not building.** Validate by selling a manual
version of your solution first.

## Step 1: Define the Problem (not the solution)

- Who specifically has this problem? (Not "businesses" — "freelance graphic designers
  who struggle with invoicing".)
- How are they solving it today? (The current workaround is your real competition.)
- How painful is this problem? (Mild annoyance vs. hair-on-fire.)
- Would they pay to make it go away?

## Step 2: Can You Solve It Manually First?

**Processizing** — create a manual valuable process before any automation:

- Do it yourself first. Write down every step on paper.
- If you can solve it manually for a few people, you can eventually automate it.
- Example: Gumroad started as Sahil manually collecting PayPal info and paying creators
  one by one.

## Step 3: Will People Pay?

The ultimate validation is a transaction:

- Can you charge for this manual service right now?
- Have you talked to at least 10 potential customers?
- Have at least 3 said they'd pay (or actually paid)?
- What price point feels natural?

## Step 4: The Four Build Questions (before building anything)

1. **Can I ship it in a weekend?** First iteration prototyped in 2–3 days. If not,
   reduce scope until you can.
2. **Is it making my customers' life a little better?** That's the bar for an MVP.
3. **Is a customer willing to pay me for it?** Profitable from day one.
4. **Can I get feedback quickly?** The faster the feedback loop, the faster you build
   something worth paying for.

## The Three Stages of Productization

1. **Manual** — you are the product; you do the work by hand for each customer.
2. **Processized** — document the process so anyone (or a vacation-cover) could do it.
   This is your "magic piece of paper".
3. **Productized** — automate each task so customers use the product without you.
   Only build what you've already proven works manually.

## MVP Scope

- **One thing.** Your product does one thing, at first.
- **No polish.** CraigsList has never been pretty. Polish comes after demand is proven.
- Most apps are just **forms and lists** (CRUD) — your MVP should be no more complex.

## Pricing (when you get there)

- Charge from day one — validation is a transaction.
- Price based on the value to the customer and what the manual version was worth, not
  cost-plus.
- If nobody flinches, you're too cheap. If nobody buys, you're too expensive (or the
  problem isn't painful enough).

## First Customers

- Sell to the community you found, personally, one by one — before marketing.
- Get feedback with every sale; iterate the manual process.
- Marketing (content, not ads) only after product-market fit (~100 customers).

## Checklist

- [ ] Specific problem + specific person named
- [ ] Current workaround identified (real competition)
- [ ] Manual/processized version delivered to someone
- [ ] 10 conversations, 3+ "would pay"
- [ ] Four build questions answered honestly
- [ ] MVP is one thing, no polish, shippable in a weekend
